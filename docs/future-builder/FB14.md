[Evidence](evidence/fb14-20261027.json) records exact sources, artifacts and scope.

behind the skyline. The clock keeps its date and the user's time-format choice.

switch. Depth changes only the clock's alpha on its existing surface. It never

## Scene lighting

The masked clock can now take light from the Ridge picture. Clock › Face shows
“Scene lighting” (Off, Soft, Rich) under the hills switch while depth works. Off
gives exactly the earlier pixels. Soft is the default because it reads as part
of the scene without changing the clock’s color identity: it tints the digits
slightly toward the sky behind them, warms them toward the horizon, darkens
them near the ridge, and adds a faint rim where the sky is bright behind an
edge. Rich makes the same changes more strongly. The recipe is in
`src/depth/lighting.rs`: ambient tint, exposure, light-wrap rim, haze of up to
14% (Rich, near the ridge), ridge contact shade, a 2.5–4% top-to-bottom sheen,
and a readability floor. The floor keeps light digits at no less than 1.5× the
local picture luminance plus 0.04, unless the plain clock was already below
that. Dark digits get the mirrored ceiling. Lighting runs after the existing `after * 100 < before * 55` fallback,
only recolors pixels the matte left visible, and never moves them.

Lighting runs in the same bounded worker: CPU only, raster at most 2048×1024,
2 s CPU and 512 MiB. The picture is cropped to the clock with a blur margin, the
glyph field is blurred at block size, and per-block terms are cached by row.
Release worker, 1920×1080 Ridge, “10:42” (CPU s and peak RSS):

| Scale | Off | Soft | Rich |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100% | 0.04 / 32 MB | 0.08 / 41 MB | 0.08 / 42 MB |
| 200% | 0.07 / 41 MB | 0.12 / 50 MB | 0.13 / 51 MB |

While depth is on but can’t work, the row shows one line saying why and one
button that fixes it. The reasons, checked in this order:

1. Not a picture: “Choose a picture…”, which opens Wallpaper’s picture chooser,
   or “Use Halcyon’s Ridge wallpaper”, applied on Wallpaper so it can be undone
   there.
2. Your own design: “Open design”.
3. Daily overview: “Turn off Daily overview”.
4. Easier to read: “Turn off Easier to read”. This comes before the card
   check because Easier to read itself adds a card.
5. Analog type: “Use digital”.
6. Background card: “Remove the card”.

Type and card fixes follow the selected moment. The lighting choice uses the
existing preference Undo. `/clock/lighting` is a widget style field, and setups
that carry it need profile format 20 (rain stays 19). Captures include it only
after it has been set, so older captures keep their version.

## Your own pictures

The hills switch now also works on the person’s own pictures. Clock › Face
shows “Let the scene cover the clock” whenever the wallpaper is a picture. On
a picture other than Ridge it needs the scene model: Depth Anything V2 Small
(`onnx-community/depth-anything-v2-small`, revision `4472b73`, Apache-2.0).
The “Scene model” row explains what it is, its size (about 99 MB) and that it
runs only on this computer, and offers Download, Cancel and Remove. The file
is fetched from that one pinned revision only. It must be exactly 99,060,839
bytes with SHA-256 `afb6a5c2…a1df10c`, or it is removed and the row says why.
It is kept under `~/.local/share/halcyon/models/` and never bundled.

Each picture is analysed once, in a bounded child (`--depth-scene-worker`):

- one at a time per picture, claimed across processes;
- 3 GiB address space, 40 s CPU and a 60 s deadline;
- four inference threads, under the existing two-job media pool;
- cancelled when the picture changes or the model is removed.

The child keeps the foreground mask, an edge map and a small working copy of
the picture. These are cached by the picture’s content (SHA-256), not its
path, under `$XDG_CACHE_HOME/halcyon/scenes`. The cache holds at most 32
pictures and 256 MiB, each file at most 48 MiB, and the least recently used
pictures go first. Removing the model clears the cache as well.

Rendering then judges the clock where it is:

- **Works:** the hills switch’s effect is on, as on Ridge, with scene lighting.
- **Needs a nudge:** the clock stays plain and the stage offers “Move to
  suggested spot”, a nearby place in the same column. The move is saved as
  one Undo step.
- **Not useful:** the clock stays plain and the row says why in one sentence
  (“This picture has no clear foreground”, for example), with “Try a different
  picture…” first and “Use Halcyon’s Ridge wallpaper instead” second. Neither
  changes the wallpaper until you choose.

While nothing is known yet, the row says “Download the scene model to use this
on your own pictures” or “Finding the foreground…”. Ridge keeps its hand-drawn
matte and is never analysed. Its pixels are byte-identical to before, which a
digest test checks.

Profiles, setups and shares carry only the existing `depth` and `lighting`
choices: no new members, no version past 20, and never a mask, cache key or
local path. A depth-on setup that arrives where its picture was never
analysed shows the plain clock. Public previews stay Ridge-only.

### Runtime choice

Both candidate runtimes ran the model in-process from Rust on five spike
pictures (Ridge, Calm, Fog and two photographs). Each picture was a 546×308
input taken from 1920×1080, given one cold run per process on this
workstation’s CPU. The ranges cover all five pictures:

| Runtime | 1 thread | 2 threads | 4 threads | Load | Peak RSS | Added to binary |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| tract-onnx 0.23.10 (`multithread-mm`) | 0.90 s | 0.56–0.57 s | 0.40–0.42 s | 0.27–0.29 s | 249–268 MB | ≈31 MB, statically linked |
| ort 2.0.0-rc.15 (ONNX Runtime, `load-dynamic`) | 0.67 s | 0.37–0.39 s | 0.26–0.28 s | 0.21–0.22 s | 251–267 MB | ≈1 MB plus a 29 MB `libonnxruntime.so` |

Both produce the same depth to within 3e-5. Preprocessing takes about
0.045 s. ort is about 1.5× faster per picture. Each picture is analysed only
once, though, so that saves about 0.15 s once per picture. tract is pure Rust:
packages need no prebuilt native library, nothing is downloaded at build time
and nothing needs to be loaded at run time. That is why it was chosen.

### Costs

- **Binary:** the release binary grows from 71,463,192 to 102,600,248 bytes
  (+31.1 MB, +44%), almost all of it tract.
- **Disk:** 99 MB for the model, once and only on request; at most 256 MiB of
  cached analyses.
- **Per picture, once:** the scene worker takes 1.28–1.40 s end to end on this
  workstation (decode, model load, inference at 546×308 for a 16:9 picture,
  mask and edges). Peak RSS is 363–390 MB; address space peaks at
  755–773 MB, well inside the 3 GiB limit.
- **Every clock render:** judging the spot and cutting the clock takes
  11–78 ms in the existing render worker. Nothing runs continuously.

A local evaluation used the 19 spike pictures that are not flat, compared
against verdicts made by eye. It was a release build, starting each clock at
its automatic place. The product agrees on usable versus not useful for
16 of 19 pictures; counting the confidence split between works and needs a
nudge as well, 11 of 19 agree exactly. Every usable picture had a suggested
spot, and the clock was cut there. The misses:

- **Rose:** the model reads a low cloud bank as foreground, so the product
  offers a spot the eye rejects.
- **One photograph:** its foreground fills most of the picture, so the
  product says no where the eye found a narrow usable band.
- **One photograph:** it is refused for having no spot where the eye would
  nudge it.

A ragged-outline limit now turns away a figure whose outline cuts through
the digits. On Paper’s cream sky the cut clock stays readable but quiet,
because a light clock cannot get brighter than white. That is the kind of
picture where “Easier to read” or a darker look is the better choice.
